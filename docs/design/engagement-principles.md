# Healthy Engagement Principles

Shardbound Isles should earn repeat play through mastery, autonomy, meaningful discovery, and satisfying closure. It must not optimize for impaired control, FOMO, or spending pressure.

## Research basis

- Self-determination research in games associates autonomy, competence, and relatedness with enjoyment, preference, well-being, and desire for future play: [Ryan, Rigby, and Przybylski (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11031-006-9051-8).
- An experimental study found that player-chosen difficulty progression improved engagement and motivation compared with the same imposed progression: [Andrieux et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.humov.2016.08.005).
- Feedback and flexible rules can support competence, while social context supports relatedness and enjoyment: [Rogers (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2017.03.048).
- WHO defines gaming disorder around impaired control, gaming displacing other activities, and continued play despite harm. Engagement design must avoid deliberately cultivating those outcomes: [WHO gaming disorder guidance](https://www.who.int/standards/classifications/frequently-asked-questions/gaming-disorder).
- Paid random-reward spending is associated with problem-gambling severity; Shardbound Isles therefore keeps procedural loot earned through play and excludes paid loot boxes: [Zendle and Cairns (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0206767).

## Prioritized product improvements

1. **Decision-first shard rewards.** After meaningful encounters, show three deterministic island-shard choices with one clear benefit, one risk, and a visible adjacency preview. This strengthens autonomy and reinforces “the world itself is loot.”
2. **A visible 15-minute mastery arc.** Present the next meaningful sequence—gather, craft, defeat, choose shard, install—without turning it into a compulsory checklist. Each step should visibly change capability or the world and end at a clean save point.
3. **Opt-in challenge bands.** Let players choose shard/rift threat tiers with explicit enemy rules and reward classes. Never secretly scale away mastery; higher challenge should be voluntary and readable.
4. **Behavior-changing build discoveries.** Expand combinations where equipment, island modifiers, automation, and adjacency alter positioning, targeting, harvesting, or production. Prefer new decisions over percentage inflation.
5. **Fast, causal feedback.** Every resource break, critical hit, legendary trigger, production completion, and adjacency activation should have a distinct short audiovisual response tied to the exact cause. Feedback must remain bounded under dense combat.
6. **World memory and relatedness.** Add a small cast of island inhabitants who recognize installed biomes, completed encounters, and production milestones. Relationships remain offline and authored; they do not become mandatory chores.
7. **Discovery collections with utility.** A biome/modifier codex should reveal what the player has personally encountered and provide practical synergy hints. Completion rewards should unlock combinations or cosmetics, not demand repetitive filler.
8. **Respectful session closure.** Expeditions should end with an explicit result summary, autosave, and “safe to stop” moment. Avoid daily streaks, expiring rewards, appointment mechanics, infinite claim chains, and punishment for taking breaks.

## Evaluation

Playtests should measure time to first meaningful choice, whether players can explain benefit/risk without prompting, voluntary build changes, perceived competence/autonomy, confusing pauses, and whether players can stop at a natural checkpoint. Raw session length is not a success metric by itself.
